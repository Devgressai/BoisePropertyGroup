## What I could NOT source, and left out

- **Who is a "relative".** 12 U.S.C. 1701j-3(d)(5) and 12 CFR 1024.31 both say
  "a transfer to a relative resulting from the death of a borrower" and neither
  defines the word. The implementing regulation (12 CFR part 191) and the CFPB's
  official interpretations of 1024.31 were NOT read. The guide says the section
  does not define it and stops. A non-relative heir, a friend, an LLC or a trust
  beneficiary is not addressed.
- **Whether a given loan is assumable, and on what terms.** That turns on the
  note and on investor or insurer rules (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA, VA, USDA).
  None of those guides were read. The page says only that it depends on the loan
  and who owns or insures it.
- **What "assumes the mortgage loan obligation under State law" means in Idaho.**
  Regulation X uses the phrase; no Idaho statute on loan assumption was found or
  read. Not described.
- **Idaho liability of a surviving spouse.** Community property and community
  debt rules (Title 32) were not researched for this page. The guide flags that a
  spouse who signed the note, a co-borrower and a married couple's debts are
  outside it. This is the biggest hole a reader could fall into.
- **Whether the estate must keep paying, and from what money.** 15-3-814 lets a
  personal representative pay an encumbrance "if it appears to be for the best
  interest of the estate". Nothing read says the estate must. Not stated.
- **Whether a deficiency after a trustee's sale can reach an estate or an heir.**
  15-3-104 mentions "any deficiency judgment which might be sought", and the
  creditor-claim deadlines (15-3-803) could matter. Not analysed; the guide says
  it is a question for an attorney.
- **How a house passes without a will, by a residuary gift, or outside probate**
  (joint tenancy, community property with right of survivorship, transfer on
  death deed, trust). 15-2-609 covers a specific devise only, and the guide says
  so. I fetched 15-3-101 (devolution at death) and 15-3-809 (secured claims) but
  made no claims from them: 15-3-101 is long and easy to misstate, and 15-3-809 is
  about how a secured claim is paid from the estate, which invites advice.
- **Response deadlines for a successor's information request.** 12 CFR
  1024.36(i)(1) applies paragraphs (c) through (g) of that section to the request.
  Those paragraphs are in the cached page but I did not claim them, so the guide
  gives no day count.
- **Regulation Z.** 12 CFR 1026.2(a)(11) (a confirmed successor in interest is a
  "consumer" for periodic statements and payoff statements), 1026.20(b),
  1026.36(c)(3) and 1026.43 were not read. The payoff-statement right is sourced
  only through 12 CFR 1024.32(c)(1)(v), which cross-refers to it.
- **Loss mitigation for a successor.** A confirmed successor is "considered a
  borrower" for the subpart, which includes 1024.41, but the official
  interpretation on how 1024.41 treats successors was not read. The guide does not
  say a successor is entitled to be reviewed for a modification. It quotes the
  CFPB's suggestion to ask about one.
- **Life insurance, mortgage protection insurance, credit life.** Not sourced, not
  mentioned.
- **Reverse mortgages.** Out of scope (separate page). The guide only notes that
  reverse mortgages are outside parts of Regulation X.
- **Federal income tax** (basis, cancelled debt). Out of scope, not mentioned.

## Traps a reviewer should check

1. **The Garn-St Germain exemption is narrow, and the prose must stay narrow.** It
   stops a lender from calling the loan due because of the transfer. It does not
   stop foreclosure for nonpayment, does not make the heir the borrower and does
   not grant a right to assume. The statute's own word on assumption is
   "encouraged" (subsection (b)(3)), and that sentence sits in the general rule on
   exercising the clause, not in the death exemption. Claim
   `im-garn-assumption-encouraged-not-required` is MEDIUM for that reason. An
   editor shortening section 2 must not drop the "limits" paragraph.
2. **"Not liable for the mortgage debt" comes from the content of an OPTIONAL
   notice** (12 CFR 1024.32(c)(1)(ii)) addressed to a successor "who is not liable
   on the mortgage loan obligation", plus a background sentence in a 2014 CFPB
   interpretive rule. Neither is an Idaho rule and neither covers someone who
   signed the note. The guide attributes both and carries the second half of the
   sentence (the lender keeps its lien and right to foreclose). An attorney should
   read section 5.
3. **The 2014 interpretive rule (79 FR 41631) is old.** I confirmed its text on
   federalregister.gov. I did NOT confirm that it is still the Bureau's position
   or how it now sits in Regulation Z's commentary. The guide dates it to 2014 and
   tells the reader to confirm its status. Both claims from it are MEDIUM. If the
   owner wants a cleaner page, section 6 paragraph 2 can be cut without breaking
   anything else.
4. **The December 2024 CFPB issue spotlight** is an agency report from a prior
   leadership period. It was live at its /data-research/ URL on 2026-11-13. It
   reports complaints ("Homeowners report being incorrectly told"), not findings.
   The prose says "reported complaints". It also refers to "mortgage program
   guidelines" I did not read. Both claims MEDIUM. Re-open the URL before each
   refresh; if it moves to /archive/, reconsider.
5. **12 CFR 1024.38 is a policies-and-procedures rule** with the word "promptly"
   and no day count, and small servicers are exempt (existing `af-regx-scope`).
   The guide says "servicers covered by that section". Do not let an edit turn it
   into "the servicer must confirm you within N days".
6. **Two different "successor in interest" terms.** Regulation X defines one;
   Idaho Code 45-1506 uses the same words for notice and cure. The guide says they
   are different definitions. I did not research whether an heir is a "successor
   in interest" under 45-1506 before a deed or letters are of record. Section 8
   only repeats the existing claims' wording ("whose interest is of record ... or
   is actually known").
7. **15-2-609 is quoted for "the mortgage stays attached"** but covers only a
   specific devise. The first section says so in the next sentence. The broader
   statement in intro[0] ("the lien stays on the house and the payments keep
   falling due") rests on 15-2-609, 15-3-812, 15-3-814 and the CFPB spotlight read
   together. It is a fair summary, but it is a summary. An attorney should confirm
   it is safe as the lead sentence.
8. **15-3-104 and 15-3-812 are described, not applied.** The guide does not say a
   lender may foreclose before probate opens. It quotes that the claims procedure
   "has no application" to a secured creditor enforcing its security. An attorney
   should confirm the description does not overstate.
9. **Probate bill S1240 (effective 01/01/2027).** I did not re-run the 2026
   session index. I relied on the note already in the registry
   (`pb-letters-required`) that S1240 replaces chapter 5 and amends 15-1-201 only.
   None of 15-2-609, 15-3-104, 15-3-812, 15-3-814 showed a future-dated banner.
   Someone should confirm S1240 does not touch chapter 2 part 6 or chapter 3 part 8.
10. **The federal 120 days and Idaho's 120 days** both appear in section 8. The
    prose says they are separate and measured from different events, per the trap
    notes on the existing claims.
11. **Business statements.** The last section uses only the owner-confirmed facts.
    It links /sell-inherited-house-idaho; confirm that path is live at merge.
12. **Wording nits.** intro[1] and the final section say "a HUD-approved housing
    counselor costs nothing"; the supporting claim says such agencies provide
    foreclosure prevention counseling free of charge. If the editor prefers, change
    to "foreclosure prevention counseling from a HUD-approved agency is free".
