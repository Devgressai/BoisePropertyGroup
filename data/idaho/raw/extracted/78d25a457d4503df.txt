COMMISSION APPROVES GEM STATE WATER APPLICATION TO CHANGE RATES
The Idaho Public Utilities Commission has approved an application from Gem State Water to change the rates it
charges customers.
Gem State Water asked for approval to change the monthly rates it charges to provide water service to customers
based on the diameter of their water meter, how much water they use and the water system they belong to. For
example, Spirit Lake East customers who have a one-inch meter (including customers formerly of the Lynnwood Estates
Subdivision), will see a monthly increase from $25.55 to $35.00. Bar Circle “S” customers (including customers
formerly of Diamond Bar), will see a decrease from $41.00 per month to $35.00 per month. Customers who have
larger water meters will pay more than those with smaller meters.
The monthly charge covers the use of a certain number of gallons. If customers use additional water, their bill will
increase. The amount of the increase depends on which water system customers are a part of.
Additional information is available at: https://puc.idaho.gov/case/Details/6894.

EASTERN IDAHO WATER UTILITY FINED FOR VIOLATING IDAHO PUBLIC UTILITY
LAWS
The Idaho Public Utilities Commission (Commission) has found that the Island Park Water Company (Company) has
failed to provide its customers with safe, reliable, and adequate service.
The Company, which serves more than 300 customers in Fremont County, is being penalized $434,000 for failure to
provide safe and reliable service, improperly billing customers, retaliating against customers who contacted the utility
with concerns about service and failing to respond to requests from the Commission for records, among other
violations. The Commission provided the Company the opportunity to avoid the imposition of the penalty if it rectified
each violation which resulted in a penalty within 21 days of the issuance of the Commission’s Order.
At a customer hearing in late March, the Commission received testimony from customers who said the Company
demanded additional payments to maintain water service, charged customers for prior owner’s bills and failed to
make repairs, among other concerns.
The Company consists of seven water systems. They are the Aspen Subdivision, Goose Bay Estates Public Water
System, Shotgun Cherokee Subdivision 5, Shotgun Kickapoo Subdivision 6 Public Water System, Shotgun North Public
Water System, Shotgun South Stevens Lane, and the Valley View Subdivision.
Additional potential penalties include the revocation of the Company’s Certificate of Public Convenience and
Necessity.
Any person interested in this Order may petition for reconsideration within twenty-one (21) days of the service date
of the Order.
Additional information is available at puc.idaho.gov/case/Details/6978/.

COMMISSION ISSUES DECISION IN VEOLIA WATER IDAHO RATE CASE
The Idaho Public Utilities Commission approved a rate increase of 7.06 percent on Friday in the rate case application
submitted by Veolia Water Idaho.
Residential customer bills will increase by an average of $2.21 per month, depending on how much water is used. The
increase will take effect on May 1, 2023.
Veolia originally asked for a 24.1 percent increase when it filed its application in September of 2022.
In its ruling, the Commission established a rate base for Veolia of $255,162,220, and a revenue requirement of
$56,157,933. The rate base for a utility is the total of the investor-funded plant, facilities, and other investments used
by a utility to provide service. The revenue requirement is the total amount of money a utility needs to collect from
customers to pay its costs, including a return on its investments.
The Commission also approved a 9.25 percent return on equity, and an overall 6.91 percent rate of return. The 6.91
percent rate of return represents an increase of $2,756,227 for Veolia. This amount represents the 7.06 percent
increase.
Veolia, which serves approximately 105,000 customers in Ada County, said it needed additional revenue to recover
increased operating expenses and costs associated with plant additions.
