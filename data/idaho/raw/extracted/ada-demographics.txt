                           Ada County Population
According to COMPASS estimates, Ada County’s population reached 557,590 in 2024, with the
majority (88.6%) living in one of the County’s six cities. Boise is the largest city with an estimated
population of 250,060, accounting for 44.8% of the County’s total residents. The next largest
city is Meridian with 142,830 residents, followed by Eagle (36,300), Kuna (31,490), Star (19,920),
and Garden City (13,380). Approximately 63,610 residents (11.4%) live outside city limits within
unincorporated areas of the County.

Between 2000 and 2024 Ada County’s population grew by 85.3% from 300,904 to 557,590, with
an average annual growth rate of 3.6%. Most of this growth occurred within the County’s cities
which together grew by 244,388 people (97.9%), with an average annual growth rate of 4.1%.
The population of unincorporated Ada County grew by 12,298 (24.0%) over this same period
with an average annual growth rate of 1.0%. The slower growth rate in unincorporated Ada
County is largely a result of the gradual reduction of unincorporated land over time through
annexations.

                                              POPULATION OF UNINCORPORATED ADA COUNTY

Only 11.4% (63,610) of Ada County’s 557,590 residents were estimated to live in unincorporated
parts of the County in 2024. Of those living in unincorporated Ada County, 78.8% (50,143) are
estimated to live within an Area of City Impact (ACI). Only approximately 13,467 people live in
unincorporated areas of the County outside of ACIs, making up approximately 2.4% of the
County’s total population, with many of these residents living in the planned communities of
Avimor, Cartwright Ranch, Dry Creek Ranch and Hidden Springs.

By 2050, COMPASS projects that Ada County will grow by approximately 28.4% (158,230) to a
population of 715,820. Of the six cities, Boise is projected to see the largest increase with 64,460
additional residents forecast by 2050. Much of this increase would be the result of Boise
annexing its Southwest Planning Area, currently located within its ACI. The city with the next
largest increase is expected to be Meridian, which is projected to add 49,710 new residents,
followed by Kuna (40,530), Eagle (18,370), Garden City (5,620), and Star (2,460).

Unincorporated Ada County is projected to see a decrease of 22,920 residents by 2050, based
on the assumption the cities will have annexed all of the area within their current ACI
boundaries by that time. The average annual rate of growth in the County as a whole
between 2024 and 2050 is expected to be 1.1%, compared to a growth rate of 3.6% between
2000 and 2024. The cities expected to see the largest average annual growth rates between
2024 and 2050 are Kuna, with a projected rate of 5.0% and Eagle with a rate of 1.9%.

Ada County’s racial and ethnic makeup became more diverse from 2000 to 2022. According
to U.S. Census Bureau data, 86.2% of Ada County residents identified as White in 2022,
compared to 92.9% in 2000. The next largest race or ethnicity was Hispanic or Latino, increasing
from 4.5% to 8.9%. The group with the largest percentage increase from 2000 to 2022 were
those of two or more races, growing from 2.2% to 6.8% of Ada County’s total population.

The percentage of Ada County residents aged 65 and over (16.3%) was slightly lower than that
of Idaho (17.0%) and the United States (17.3%) in 2022. However, the share of Ada County
residents within this age group increased at a faster rate than that of Idaho and the U.S.
between 2000 and 2022. All of Ada County’s cities experienced a significant increase within
this age group from 2000 to 2022, and as with median age, there is great variation among the
cities. Only 9.8% of Kuna residents are aged 65 or over, compared to 25.4% of Garden City
residents.
                                          MIGRATION

The U.S. Census Bureau estimates that in 2020, approximately 32,954 people moved into Ada
County, and approximately 25,475 people moved out. The actual number or residents moving
out of Ada County is likely slightly higher due to the Census Bureau not having data on the
number of Ada County residents moving to other countries. Of the residents estimated to have
moved to Ada County in 2020, 67.1% came from other states, 28.0% came from other counties
within Idaho, and 4.9% came from other countries.

The states with the highest estimated number of residents moving to Ada County were
California (7,699), Washington (2,630), Texas (1,903), and Oregon (1,645), together accounting
for 42.1% of all new residents in 2020. Among residents moving to Ada County from within
Idaho, the most popular county of origin was neighboring Canyon County (3,357), followed by
Bannock County (1,162) and Twin Falls County (734).

Among residents leaving Ada County, over half (50.7%) stayed within Idaho. For those staying
in Idaho, Canyon County was by far the most popular destination accounting for 25.7% of all
move-outs, followed by Bannock County (3.7%) and Bonneville County (2.7%). Among
residents leaving Ada County for other states, Washington was the most popular destination
accounting for 6.7% of all moves outside the County, followed by Oregon (6.1%), California
(5.9%), and Utah (3.9%).
