# Treasure Valley Zoning Intelligence

Research artifact. Governs what zoning claims commercial pages may make.

## Boise — height limits vary by an order of magnitude

Ten of Boise's 30 districts have been read. The height picture alone is a
genuine differentiator, because it is not uniform and is not intuitive:

| District | Building height maximum |
|---|---|
| **MX-5** Mixed-Use Downtown | **none stated** |
| **I-3** Industrial: Technology | **150 ft** [footnote 3, unread] |
| I-1 Industrial: Light | 55 ft |
| I-2 Industrial: Heavy | 55 ft |
| R-3 Residential Urban | 4 storeys, not to exceed 50 ft |
| MX-2 Mixed-Use General | 45 ft |

**MX-5 stating no maximum is the outlier**, and unlike R-3's density cell —
which reads `N./A` with footnotes `[2][3]` — the MX-5 height cell carries **no
footnote marker**. That is why MX-5 is recorded at HIGH confidence and R-3's
apparent lack of a density cap is withheld.

⚠️ "No maximum in the district" is not "build any height". Design review, the
Capitol Boulevard Design Overlay and the Airport Influence Area all sit outside
what has been read.

## Height is the wrong single variable for industrial

I-3 permits nearly three times the height of I-1 — but buys it with setbacks
that grow with the building:

| | I-1 / I-2 | I-3 |
|---|---|---|
| Front setback | 20 ft | 45 ft **+ 1 ft per foot of height above 45 ft**, or 100 ft |
| Interior side | 0 ft [1] | follows front |
| Rear | 0 ft [1] | follows front |
| Street frontage min | none / 30 ft (I-2) | 30 ft |
| Parking setback at I-84/I-184 | 10 ft / 20 ft | 20 ft |

A taller I-3 building consumes far more of its parcel. **Comparing districts on
height alone compares the wrong variable.**

I-3 is also benchmarked against the **mixed-use** districts rather than against
I-1/I-2 — the code positions it as the step up from mixed-use, not as a tier of
heavy industrial.

## Overlays stack; they do not replace

Boise runs ten overlays. A parcel carries a base district **and** every overlay
covering it.

Directly relevant to commercial and development ground:
- **Airport Influence Area (AI-O)** — much of Boise's industrial land is near the
  airport. Purpose: compatibility with safe airport operation and with noise.
- **Flood Protection Overlay** — the city's stated rationale recognises periodic
  inundation and that construction practice itself increases flood heights.
- Boise River System, Hillside Development, Wildland Urban Interface.

⚠️ **Only purpose clauses have been read for AI-O and Flood Protection.** No page
may state what either overlay *requires*.

## MX-4 is an expanding district — a freshness hazard

Boise states MX-4 "will be expanded as new investments in best-in-class transit
routes are added and transit stations identified."

The map is expected to change. Any page naming MX-4's extent will go stale.
Classified PERIODIC revalidation; never state where MX-4 currently applies.

## A maximum setback is not a minimum

MX-2 and MX-5 both set front and side-street setbacks as a **range: minimum 0,
maximum 20 ft**. A maximum setback pushes buildings *toward* the street rather
than merely keeping them off it, which constrains where surface parking can go —
a real consideration for retail and owner-user commercial.
