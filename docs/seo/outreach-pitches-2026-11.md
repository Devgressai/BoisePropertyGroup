## 1. Idaho Capital Sun — Idaho's non-disclosure status, five years on

**To:** info@idahocapitalsun.com (general desk). Laura Guido covered housing in June 2026.
**Hook:** In 2021 they reported that Idaho is one of 10 states with no obligation to disclose a property's sale price. Here's the 2026 follow-up angle.

> Subject: Idaho still can't see its own home prices — a 2026 follow-up to your 2021 story
>
> In August 2021 the Capital Sun reported that Idaho is one of the states with no
> obligation to disclose a property's sale price. Five years and a large run-up in
> Treasure Valley prices later, the mechanics are unchanged and worth a second look:
>
> - Recorder records are fully public and free to inspect (Idaho Code 31-2419).
>   The price simply is not one of the elements a deed must contain (55-601).
> - County assessors describe Idaho, in their own words, as a non-disclosure state
>   that depends on the public to report sales.
> - The assessor's value is tied to a 1 January lien date (63-205) and has to sit
>   within 90–110% of market by category (63-208), but each property is only
>   reappraised once every five years (63-314).
>
> We've put the statutes and the assessor statements together, each linked to its source:
> https://boisepropertygroup.com/commercial/how-value-is-determined
>
> Happy to walk through it. [Name], Boise Property Group
